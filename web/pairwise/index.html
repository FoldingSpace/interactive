A comparison says how much more important one of the two is than the other. The scale runs from 1, which is the same, up to 9.

The minus button moves towards the name on the left. The plus button moves towards the name on the right. Arrow keys do the same.

On a computer you can also click the row of ticks to jump straight to a position.

Set all three at once

Each button sets all three comparisons at once. The link in the address bar then holds the numbers it set, so a setting can be sent to somebody else.

For the classroom

On your own, one minute, on paper. Somebody is choosing where to put a bench in a park. Three things matter: shade, view and quiet. They say shade matters clearly more than quiet. Shade matters a little more than view. View and quiet matter about the same. Write down the three percentages you think those sentences come to, largest first. Do this before you open the page.

In pairs, two minutes, one phone between two. Rename the three criteria to shade, view and quiet. Set each comparison to the numbers each of you wrote, one of you after the other.

Back to the room, two minutes. Whose sentences were these? Who else could have been asked?

A comparison says how much more important one criterion is than another. A criterion is one of the three things being compared.

The three weights come from the three comparisons. They are shares of one whole, so they add up to 100 per cent.

The consistency ratio measures the three comparisons against each other. It is 0 when the three fit together exactly. The further they are from fitting, the larger it gets.
