Interactive widgets

Small pages that show one idea from cartography or geographic information science, and let you change something to see what happens. They run in a browser with nothing to install, on a phone or on a projector.

  • Spatial autocorrelation on a grid

    Paint black and white squares, choose which neighbours count, and watch Moran’s I respond — globally and square by square.

  • Drawing the lines

    Cut Vancouver into areas one way, then another. The records stay the same and the answer does not. The modifiable areal unit problem, and what taking space seriously does and does not fix, on police-reported incidents and census data.

  • Least cost, whose cost?

    Say what crossing farmland, houses, parks or water is worth avoiding, and a power line finds its own way across Surrey. Draw new land on the map and it finds another. Keep the routes, and see how many others cost almost the same.
